# Candidates — The Funniest Excuse for Skipping a Meeting

Brief: one razor-sharp line (max 200 chars) that excuses you from a pointless
work meeting or Zoom call. The judge pairs entries head to head and keeps the
sharper, more relatable laugh. Stock excuses (bad wifi, a cat, a dentist)
explicitly lose, so every candidate below avoids them.

## 1. ⭐ FINAL PICK

> Sorry, I have to skip — I read the agenda and my will to live filed a formal complaint with HR.
> (95 characters)

**Why it wins:** three beats that escalate — a polite opener, then the
surprising turn ("my will to live"), then the bureaucratic punchline ("filed
a formal complaint with HR"). Dark, specific, instantly true to office life:
everyone has read an agenda and felt their soul leave. Tight at 95 chars,
no wasted words, and a completely fresh angle — no tech failure, no pet, no
appointment. The HR detail makes the despair sound like paperwork, which is
exactly why a room of office workers laughs and nods at the same time.

## 2.

> I already attended this meeting in 2019, 2021, and last Tuesday. Nothing changed, so neither will I.
> (100 characters)

**Why it's strong:** the list of years is a funny, specific exaggeration and
"so neither will I" is a clean punchline. Relatability is very high —
recurring meetings that never change are universal. Loses to #1 only because
its turn is a neat wordplay rather than a surprising image.

## 3.

> Can't join — Outlook just booked me into a mandatory 1:1 with my own burnout.
> (77 characters)

**Why it's strong:** turns corporate scheduling language against itself; a
"mandatory 1:1 with my own burnout" is a fresh, funny image. Loses to #1
because the laugh depends on knowing Outlook/1:1 jargon, and the turn is
gentler.

## 4.

> Skipping — the meeting is 60 minutes, the decision inside it takes twelve seconds, and I already made it.
> (105 characters)

**Why it's strong:** the quantified absurdity (60 minutes vs twelve seconds)
is sharp and true, and "I already made it" is a confident closer. Loses to
#1 because it reads more clever than funny — a smirk, not a laugh.

## 5.

> My calendar auto-declined after detecting the words 'quick sync' for the fifth time today.
> (90 characters)

**Why it's strong:** personifies the calendar as an ally that has had enough;
"quick sync" is a perfect trigger phrase. Loses to #1 because the joke is
one-note — the calendar did the rebelling, not the person.

## 6.

> I'm still stuck in the meeting about the meeting we had about the meeting, so I'll have to miss this one.
> (105 characters)

**Why it's strong:** the recursion is immediately recognizable and the
self-reference is elegant. Loses to #1 because "meeting about the meeting"
is a well-worn office joke — the judge rewards fresh angles.

## 7.

> I tried to join, but my camera saw the agenda and turned itself off out of respect for us both.
> (95 characters)

**Why it's strong:** a device with better judgment than its owner is a funny
image, and "out of respect for us both" is a polite little twist. Loses to
#1 because it leans on the tired camera-off trope.

## 8.

> Sorry — my laptop and I had a 1:1 about my engagement, and we both agreed this meeting isn't it.
> (96 characters)

**Why it's strong:** weaponizes HR-speak ("1:1", "engagement") against the
meeting itself. Loses to #1 because the punchline is a bit abstract — "isn't
it" lands softer than a formal complaint to HR.

## 9.

> Can't make it — I'm on hold with IT because my soul keeps buffering every time someone says 'circle back'.
> (106 characters)

**Why it's strong:** "my soul keeps buffering" is a vivid image and 'circle
back' is a real trigger phrase. Loses to #1 because it mixes two jokes (IT
hold + buffering soul) and the tech framing is close to the banned wifi
family.

## 10.

> I won't be there — the meeting has no agenda, no decision, and no end, which is three strikes in one invite.
> (108 characters)

**Why it's strong:** the rule-of-three structure is tight and the "three
strikes" closer is neat. Loses to #1 because it argues the case instead of
landing a laugh — more complaint than comedy.
